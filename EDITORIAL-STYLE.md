# BioScope editorial standard

Write for intelligent non-specialists, not industry insiders. Preserve scientific precision, but give readers enough context and narrative flow to understand why the news matters.

This standard applies to every new article and every revision of existing reporting. It supersedes earlier preferences for compressed clinical or financial summaries.

## Reusable drafting instruction

Using only verified sources available by the edition cutoff, write objective, accessible journalism for educated general readers. Start with what happened and why it matters. Establish the patient population, treatment comparison and trial stage before detailed clinical results. For business reporting, explain the strategic significance beyond the financial headline. End with the next evidenced development or unresolved question; do not invent a forecast.

Aim for 200–300 words and 4–6 coherent paragraphs for a typical major news story. Adjust for complexity and available evidence; do not pad to a quota. Analysis may be longer and retain its useful existing structure. Use complete sentences, logical transitions and one main idea per paragraph. Avoid abstract-like compression, promotional language, textbook exposition and unexplained abbreviations. Explain specialized concepts briefly within the narrative when needed to follow the story. Do not define every basic scientific term.

Retain important figures, drug and company names, comparisons, safety findings and uncertainties. Distinguish relative from absolute effects, response from survival, early signals from randomized evidence, and regulatory review from approval. Separate reported facts, company claims and explicitly identified interpretation. For transactions, distinguish upfront cash, conditional milestones, royalties and acquisition values. Never add unsupported background, causal claims or outcomes for narrative effect.

Preserve citations and source URLs. Cite paragraphs to the evidence supporting their factual statements. Respect source quotation and summarization limits; obtain additional independent evidence when needed rather than expanding a single source beyond permitted use. Preserve edition dates, images, routes, relationships and design unless the user requests otherwise. Recalculate reading time from the revised body.

## Editorial review before publication

Read the draft as a curious reader without industry training. Can they explain what happened, why it matters and what happens next? Check that background precedes difficult findings, abbreviations are understandable, statistics have denominators/comparators, and transitions connect the paragraphs. Remove filler and repeated caveats without removing material limitations. Compare every numerical and regulatory statement with the sources. Then run the normal production and browser checks.

## Publishing workflow

This is a static site with agent-authored edition records, not an autonomous article-generation service. Agents and any future automation that drafts articles must read this file before writing or editing edition data. Use the reusable drafting instruction above as the generation prompt, then perform the editorial review. Build validation cannot judge prose quality or factual accuracy and does not replace that review.
